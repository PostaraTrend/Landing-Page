Verified beats volume.

Most job search tools help you apply to more roles, faster, with a resume that says whatever the posting wants to hear. Role Scout does the opposite. It helps you send one resume an employer can check, line by line, against what you attested. This page shows the difference side by side, and what an employer sees when you share the link.

Verified bannerAn independent check confirmed that every line is supported by the candidate's attested lines and that the system invented nothing.
VersionWhich attested version of the resume the check ran against. A candidate who updates their resume attests it again and the version number goes up.
Provenance countsHow many lines were kept, reworded or merged, and how many were added. Added is the number to watch, and it is always zero.

What it does not claimVerification is a statement about provenance, not a background check. It proves the tailored resume matches what the candidate attested. Whether the attested history is true remains the candidate's word, which is exactly where it should sit.
Why it matters now

Employers have stopped taking resumes at face value.

Hiring teams say AI enhanced resumes have made skills harder to verify, and verification products are being built into the hiring process. In that world, the applicant who can be checked is the applicant who gets the call.

For the seeker

You stop competing on volume, which you cannot win, and start competing on trust, which nobody else in the pile can offer.

For the employer

One link replaces the first round of doubt. What is on the page is what the candidate attested, checked, with the counts to prove it.

For the people who help seekers

Newcomer centres and career offices can put a verified application in front of an employer without vouching for what they cannot see. The link does the vouching.

Fair questions

Asked plainly, answered plainly.

Does verified mean the candidate is telling the truth?

It means the tailored resume matches what the candidate attested, line by line, with nothing added by the system. The attested history itself is the candidate's own statement, the same as any resume. Verification removes the tool from the list of things you have to doubt.

Can a candidate edit the resume after it is verified?

Yes, and the verification page will say so. The banner returns only after the resume is tailored and checked again against the attested lines.

Does Role Scout apply on the candidate's behalf?

No, by design. The candidate applies on your site, in person, with a resume made for your role. Nothing is submitted automatically anywhere.

What can I see on the verification page?

The tailored resume, the verified banner, the version it was checked against and the provenance counts. The candidate's source resume is never shown, and sharing is off until the candidate turns it on.

Is there a cost to employers?

No. Opening a verification link is free and needs no account.
